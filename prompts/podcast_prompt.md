Make this the longest, most thorough Deep Dive you can — 45 minutes or more — for an MBA student preparing for a Harvard Business School class. The listener is walking or driving and cannot take notes: say the important things twice in different words, and signpost every transition ("we're now on page six", "second discussion question").

Sources and how to treat them:
- The CASE and any READINGS are the primary material: the facts, the story, the numbers.
- The source titled "CHEAT SHEET" is the student's own prepared analysis. It is NOT the case. Every time you draw on it, say so ("the cheat sheet argues…", "the prep notes work this out as…") so the listener always knows what the case states versus what the analysis concludes. Never present a cheat-sheet conclusion as a fact from the case.
- The "CANVAS POSTING" source holds the professor's discussion questions.

Structure:
1. A very terse executive summary — the story, the characters, the decision — in a minute or two. Then a fuller version of the same summary with more texture: the setting, the pressures on each character, what is really at stake.
2. Walk through the case in order, page by page (or section by section): what happens in each part, why it matters, what the reader is meant to notice. Go slowly on the exhibits and explain what each one is for.
3. Numbers: focus on the handful of anchor numbers everything hinges on. For each, explain conceptually how it was arrived at and what it means — no reading out tables — and repeat the anchors when they come up again.
4. The discussion questions, one at a time: read the question, work out what the answer is and how you'd get there, and take the listener through the things to consider — the tensions, the counter-arguments, what a strong classroom answer sounds like. Use the cheat sheet's reasoning here, labelled as such, and push on it where it is weak or where the case supports a different view.
5. Close with the three things to remember walking into class.

Style: two hosts in a substantive conversation at the level of a strong HBS second-year; depth and case specificity over generality; HBS case discussion conventions.
