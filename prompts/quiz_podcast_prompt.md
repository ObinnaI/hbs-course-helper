Make this the longest, most thorough review episode the format allows: aim for 40 minutes or more and never under 30. It prepares an MBA student for a short multiple-choice quiz that tests definitions, lists and headline results exactly as the professor presented them. The listener is walking and cannot take notes: say every definition twice, once in the professor's words and once in plain words, and signpost every transition.

Sources and how to treat them:
- The source titled "STUDY GUIDE" is the student's prepared guide. It tells you what is in scope, which terms matter, and it contains a practice quiz. Follow its order.
- The slide decks are the professor's own slides and are the authority on wording. The readings and role sheets fill in detail.

Structure:
1. Two minutes: what this quiz covers, how the professor's quizzes work (five quick multiple-choice questions, definitional, often one "which is NOT" question), and the plan for the episode.
2. Review, about half the episode. Go through the key terms in the guide's order. For each: the term, the definition in the professor's words, the plain-words version, an example from the class exercise, and the tempting wrong answer. Then read each list from the slides slowly, item by item, and name the fake items a "which is NOT" question might use. Then the named studies: who, when, what they found, the number to remember. Do not skip a term because it seems obvious; the obvious ones are what gets asked.
3. Drill, about half the episode. One host is the quizmaster. For each practice question in the study guide: read the stem, read the four options clearly, say "take a second", pause, then give the answer and explain in one sentence each why the other three are wrong. Do at least twenty questions, mixing in the "which is NOT" ones. Every few questions, circle back to a term that was missed-prone.
4. Earlier material: three minutes on the earlier terms still fair game, fast.
5. Close by reading the one-page card: the ten definitions, the three lists, the five numbers.

Style: two hosts; brisk, precise, a little playful in the drill; never vague about a definition; no case storytelling beyond what a question could ask.
